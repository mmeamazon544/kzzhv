The Liturgical Music of the Kehillah

נְעִימוֹת זְמִירוֹת יִשְׂרָאֵל

Our Music

The melodies of the Sabbath-morning service as we sing them — woven of the Western Sephardic rite and the great traditions of Amsterdam, London, New York, Rome, and beyond — gathered here with their sources and variations, alongside gems of classical Ashkenazi nussaḥ, Ḥassidic niggun, and contemporary harmonies.

The choir of Bevis Marks Synagogue, London, singing by candlelight.

About this collection

Each tune — or each group of tunes set to the same text — has its own panel, in the order of the service. Press any play button to listen.

Where more than one recording is gathered for a single text, the version marked As Sung at KKZZ is the melody as we usually sing it; the others are its sources and variations — the traditions from which it grew, or renderings we enjoy and occasionally sing, for comparison.

Poems not usually found in the siddur are transcribed and translated here.

✦  None of these recordings was made on a Sabbath or a Festival.  ✦

שַׁחַר אֲבַקֶּשְׁךָ

Shaḥar Avakeshkha

“Early I Will Seek You” — a reshut (introduction) to Nishmat of Yom Kippur.

As Sung at KKZZ

The version sung daily in Amsterdam until the Second World War.

Rabbi Jonathan Cohen & family

Amsterdam

Ḥazzan Daniel Halfon

The Text & Translation

Western Sephardic Nosaḥ. Solomon ibn Gabirol (Shelomoh ben Yehudah ibn Gabirol; Avicebron), c. 1021/22, Málaga – c. 1058, Valencia (some sources give c. 1070) — Hebrew poet and Neoplatonic philosopher of al-Andalus. A reshut for Nishmat on Yom Kippur.

I’ll seek You at day’s dawning,

O my Rock Who rescues me;

I’ll unfurl my prayer before You,

in the morning and at eve.

In the morning and at eve.

שַׁחַר אֲבַקֶּשְׁךָ, צוּרִי וּמִשְׂגַּבִּי,

אֶעֱרֹךְ לְפָנֶיךָ שַׁחְרִי וְגַם עַרְבִּי.

שַׁחְרִי וְגַם עַרְבִּי.

Before Your boundless greatness

I will stand and I will quail,

for Your eyes penetrate my heart

and all my thoughts You see.

In the morning and at eve.

לִפְנֵי גְדֻלָּתְךָ אֶעֱמֹד וְאֶבָּהֵל,

כִּי עֵינְךָ תִרְאֶה כָל מַחְשְׁבוֹת לִבִּי.

שַׁחְרִי וְגַם עַרְבִּי.

What could my heart or tongue do,

existing on their own?

And what strength would I have without

Your Spirit placed in me?

In the morning and at eve.

מַה זֶּה אֲשֶׁר יוּכַל הַלֵּב וְהַלָּשׁוֹן

לַעֲשׂוֹת, וּמַה כֹּחִי רוּחִי בְּתוֹךְ קִרְבִּי?

שַׁחְרִי וְגַם עַרְבִּי.

But lo, You love the songs

of humankind; so then,

I’ll give You praise for all the days

Your Spirit dwells in me.

In the morning and at eve.

הִנֵּה לְךָ יִיטַב זִמְרַת אֱנוֹשׁ, עַל כֵּן

אוֹדְךָ בְּעוֹד תִּהְיֶה נִשְׁמַת אֱלוֹהַּ בִּי.

שַׁחְרִי וְגַם עַרְבִּי.

The Text & Translation

Yehi Shalom be-Ḥelenu is an anonymous early piyyut for berit milah, by a poet named Yehoshua (from its acrostic), first printed in Venice in 1522. We offer here versions for male and female. The Hebrew shown is the version for a boy.

May there be peace within our walls,

tranquility in Israel.

In a good sign, a child has come to us;

May this child live to see the Goel (Redeemer)!

יְהִי שָׁלוֹם בְּחֵילֵנוּ וְשַׁלְוָה בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל

בְּסִימָן טוֹב בֵּן בָּא לָנוּ בְּיָמָיו יָבֹא הַגּוֹאֵל:

May the child be fresh and flourishing;

And in the Divine Presence bask.

May the Torah for them be nourishing;

Let them teach all who care to ask.

הַיֶּלֶד יְהִי רַעֲנָן בְּצֵל שַׁדַּי יִתְלוֹנָן

וּבַתּוֹרָה אָז יִתְבּוֹנָן יְאַלֵּף דָּת לְכָל שׁוֹאֵל:

May the parents both be blessed;

may the child’s lifespan be long.

With a table always set,

with proper food for that journey long.

וּמְקוֹרוֹ יְהִי בָרוּךְ זְמַן חַיָּיו יְהִי אָרוּךְ

וְשֻׁלְחָנוֹ יְהִי עָרוּךְ וְזִבְחוֹ לֹא יִתְגָּאֵל:

May their name go forth everywhere;

when they grow may their worth be without bounds.

May they dwell with the God-aware,

Like Samuel and Yael in their time renowned.

שְׁמוֹ יֵצֵא בְּכָל עֵבֶר אֲשֶׁר יִגְדַּל יְהִי גֶבֶר

וּלְיִרְאֵי אֵל יְהִי חָבֵר יְהִי בְדוֹרוֹ כִשְׁמוּאֵל:

Until old age and full grey hair,

may they be full of every good thing.

May peace and abundant love be theirs;

Amen, let our assent ring!

עֲדֵי זִקְנָה וְגַם שֵׂיבָה יְהִי דָשֵׁן בְּכָל טוֹבָה

וְשָׁלוֹם לוֹ וְרֹב אַהֲבָה אָמֵן כֵּן יֹאמַר הָאֵל:

Concluding verse

For a boy:

May the newly-circumcised child among his people dwell, with his father and his mother. May his God be with him always, and with the whole house of Israel.

הַנִּמּוֹל בְּתוֹךְ עַמּוֹ יִחְיֶה לְאָבִיו וּלְאִמּוֹ

וְיִהְיֶה אֱלֹהָיו עִמּוֹ וְעִם כָּל בֵּית יִשְׂרָאֵל:

For a girl:

The Text & Translation

Yafutzu Oyvekha — an Italian-rite hymn for taking out or returning the Torah, built on Numbers 10:35–36; attributed to Yoav b. Yeḥiel of Rome, first printed in an Ashkenazic siddur in 5304 (1543/1544).

When the Ark sets forth Your foes will all flee, scattering

And when it rests, Your nation, then, will hymn their thanksgiving.

יָפוּצוּ אֹויְבֶיךָ בִּנְסֹועַ הָאָרֹן.

וּבְנוּחֹה עַמֶּךָ שִׁיר מִזְמֹור יָרֹן:

The Priests of Your people in vestments will appear;

Your Levites singing sweetly, their voices loud and clear.

Prized among the nations, of great praise and renown;

And when the Ark rests, let Israel’s joyous song resound.

וְכֹהֲנֵי עַמֶּךָ יִלְבְּשׁוּ רִקְמָה.

יֹאמְרוּ חֲסִידֶיךָ שִׁירָה בִּנְעִימָה.

גֹּוי עַל כָּל אֻמָּה שֶׁבַח וְיִתְרֹון.

וּבְנוּחֹה עַמֶּךָ שִׁיר מִזְמֹור יָרֹן:

Let the High Priest’s breastplate shine again as of old;

Let incense rise in perfumed clouds, with spices rare and bold:

For Aaron’s children, now anointed, to worship newly sworn.

And when it rests, let Israel’s song be born.

אוּרִים וְתֻמִּים כְּבָרִאשֹׁונָה.

קְטֹרֶת סַמִּים שְׁחֵלֶת וְחֶלְבְּנָה.

וְהָיָה לֹו לְמָנָה לְזֶרַע אַהֲרֹן.

וּבְנוּחֹה עַמֶּךָ שִׁיר מִזְמֹור יָרֹן:

For the sake of Your servant David, Yishai’s son, we pray;

Send Messiah to us and let tribute come Your way.

We will offer our gifts, a fiery oblation;

When Your Ark comes to rest, let Israel sing salvation!

בַּעֲבוּר עַבְדְּךָ דָּוִד בֶּן יִשַׁי.

שְׁלַח מְשִׁיחֶךָ וּלְךָ יֹובִילוּ שַׁי.

לְהַקְרִיב אִשֵּׁי מִנְחַת זִכָּרֹון.

וּבְנוּחֹה עַמֶּךָ שִׁיר מִזְמֹור יָרֹן:

Rhyming translation by Professor Marc M. Epstein.

Bendigamos

As Sung at KKZZ

Rabbi Shuviel Maaravi

the Cohen family

The Text & Translation

Bendigamos — the Western Sephardic grace after meals, in Spanish, sung to close the table. The refrain (“Alabado sea su santo Nombre…”) follows each verse; it ends with the Hebrew verse Hodu l’Adonai.

Bendigamos al Altísimo,

al Señor que nos crió;

démosle agradecimiento

por los bienes que nos dió.

Let us bless the Most High,

the One who raised us;

let us give thanks

for the good things we received.

Alabado sea su santo Nombre,

porque siempre nos apiadó.

Load al Señor, que es bueno,

que para siempre su merced.

Praised be the Holy Name,

for the mercy God has shown.

Give thanks to ADONAI, Who is good;

Whose Divine Love is everlasting.

Bendigamos al Altísimo,

por su Ley primeramente,

que liga a nuestra raza

con el cielo continuamente.

Let us bless the Most High,

first for the Torah,

which connects our people

with Heaven continually.

Bendigamos al Altísimo,

por el pan segundamente,

y también por los manjares

que comimos juntamente.

Let us bless the Most High,

secondly for the bread,

and also for the foods

which we have eaten together.

Pues comimos y bebimos alegremente;

su merced nunca nos faltó.

Load al Señor, que es bueno,

que para siempre su merced.

For we have eaten and drunk happily;

Divine mercy has not failed us.

Praise the One who is good,

for mercy everlasting.

Bendita sea la casa esta,

el hogar de su presencia,

donde guardamos su fiesta

con alegría y permanencia.

Blessed be this house,

the home of the Divine Presence,

where we keep the festivals

with happiness and permanence.

הוֹדוּ לַה׳ כִּי טוֹב, כִּי לְעוֹלָם חַסְדּוֹ.

Hodu l’Adonai ki tov, ki l’olam ḥasdo.

Give thanks to ADONAI, Who is good; Whose Divine Love is everlasting.

Translation by Professor Marc M. Epstein.

The Text & Translation

Tenu Shebaḥa — a Simḥat Torah hymn for the Ḥatan Torah and Ḥatan Bereshit. Lyrics and music by Reverend Jacques Judah Lyons (1814–1877), a Suriname-born Sephardic ḥazzan, rabbi, and communal leader at Shearith Israel from 1839 to 1877.

Praises we tender, songs we sing in awe,

To the Fashioner of Light and Giver of the Law;

This beloved Ḥatan / Kallah our Congregation chose

To bring the Torah’s reading to a close.

So let us rejoice with this pure soul in delight,

And may our God lift our prayers to their height,

Together with this dear friend who’s called forth by name,

To help the Torah’s glory shine as if in flame!

תְּנוּ שְׁבָחָה וְגַם שִׁירָה

לְיוֹצֵר אוֹר וּלְנוֹתֵן תּוֹרָה

בָּאִישׁ הַזֶּה הָעֵדָה בָּחֲרָה

לְהַשְׁלִים אֶת הַתּוֹרָה

אָנָּא נִשְׂמַח בְּנֶפֶשׁ טְהוֹרָה

וְנִתְפַּלֵּל לָאֵל הַנּוֹרָא

עִם הָאִישׁ אֲשֶׁר נִקְרָא

לְהוֹפִיעַ כְּבוֹד הַתּוֹרָה

בְּהַשְׁלִימוֹ אֶת הַתּוֹרָה

Praise, too, and song to God again we send,

To One with no beginning, and without end;

This is the Ḥatan / Kallah our Congregation chose

To open Torah’s reading as it flows.

So let us rejoice with this pure soul in delight,

And may our God lift our prayers to their height,

Together with this dear friend who’s called forth by name,

To help the Torah’s glory shine as if in flame!

תְּנוּ שְׁבָחָה וְגַם שִׁירָה

לִבְלִי רֵאשִׁית וּלְבְלִי תַכְלִית

בָּאִישׁ הַזֶּה הָעֵדָה בָּחֲרָה

לְהַתְחִיל אֶת הַתּוֹרָה

אָנָּא נִשְׂמַח בְּנֶפֶשׁ טְהוֹרָה

וְנִתְפַּלֵּל לָאֵל הַנּוֹרָא

עִם הָאִישׁ אֲשֶׁר נִקְרָא

לְהוֹפִיעַ כְּבוֹד הַתּוֹרָה

בְּהַתְחִילוֹ אֶת הַתּוֹרָה

Rhyming translation by Professor Marc M. Epstein.
