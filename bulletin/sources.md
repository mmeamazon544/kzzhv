Calendar: Hebcal, through its public calendar service, with the attribution line Hebcal asks for placed in the bulletin's colophon.

Texts: Sefaria, through its open API (developers.sefaria.org). The parashah and haftarah, Talmud, midrash, the commentators, the Zohar, and above all the Mishneh Torah, which is the first recourse for the halakhic teaching. Before a teaching goes into a proof, every citation in it is checked against Sefaria's catalogue; a reference that does not resolve to an actual passage is discarded and the teaching redrafted. This is the guarantee against invented sources. Sefaria citations appear in the bulletin as links.

The Koren Rosh HaShana Mahzor, Rabbi Jonathan Sacks's introduction (on Sefaria): for the Days of Awe, read for its ideas and paraphrased with attribution, not quoted at length; Koren's text is under copyright.

Keter Shem Tob of Shem Tob Gaguine, seven volumes, 1934–1981: the primary authority for Western Sephardic usage and for the comparison of customs across communities, and the natural anchor of the halakhic teaching whenever a custom is in question. ketershemtob.com carries no text of the book, only a topical index by volume and page (index1.html, volume1.html through volume7.html). The volumes are scanned on HebrewBooks.org: vols. I–II at hebrewbooks.org/14392, vol. III at 14390, vol. VI at 14391, vol. VII at 14389; find IV and V. Use the site's index to locate the pages bearing on the week's topic, read the scanned Hebrew pages, and cite by volume, page, and se'if. Sefaria's "Keter Shem Tov" is a different book, the Baal Shem Tov anthology whose standard modern edition is Kehot's; exclude it by name so the two are never confused.

Excluded from both reading and citation, everywhere and always: Chabad.org and sites that repackage it, Kehot, Sichos in English, and the Chabad literature from Shneur Zalman of Liadi onward (Tanya, Shulhan Arukh HaRav, Hayom Yom, the Rebbes' sichot and ma'amarim). Chabad.org's zemanim are not used either. Hasidic sources outside Chabad remain open: Kedushat Levi, Me'or Einayim, Noam Elimelekh, Mei HaShiloah, Sefat Emet, and the like.
