topic: Selihot in the closing days of Elul: rising before dawn, the Thirteen Attributes as a devar shebikdushah requiring a minyan, and the Western Sephardic practice of saying Selihot from 2 Elul rather than beginning on Motsa'ei Shabbat Nitzavim (this Saturday night, 5 September) as Ashkenazim do
topic: "Lo bashamayim hi" (Deut. 30:11-14): teshuvah is not distant or beyond reach but "very near to you, in your mouth and in your heart" — a fitting word for the week before Rosh Hashana

## Halakha

<p>Elul is nearly gone, and the Rambam records what these days look like in a congregation: everyone rises while it is still night and prays in the synagogues with words that break the heart, until daybreak.<sup>1</sup> He also rules that repentance and calling out are welcome at every hour of the year, and that in the days near Rosh Hashana they are welcomed more readily still; a community that turns with a whole heart is answered at once.<sup>2</sup> Selihot are that rising put into words. The hour is early, the room is dark, and the prayer is public.</p><p>The Sephardic custom is to rise in the last watch of the night from Rosh Hodesh Elul until Kippur, while Ashkenazim begin on the Sunday before Rosh Hashana, and a week earlier when Rosh Hashana falls early in the week.<sup>3</sup> London and Amsterdam long said Selihot in the evening, before Kadish Titkabbal, since few could rise before dawn in these cold countries and more people came to the synagogue at minha and arbit; the Hida objected sharply, holding that the Thirteen Attributes belong to an hour of favour.<sup>4</sup> The Attributes wait for a minyan. Ours is made of women and men, counted alike.</p>

## Aggada

<p>The Ramban takes “this commandment” in Moses' mouth to be teshuvah itself. Nothing about it is hard or far off. It can be done at any time and in any place: the mouth confesses, the heart turns.<sup>1</sup> The midrash makes the same point as a question. They asked Moses: you tell us it is not in the heavens and not across the sea; then where is it? He answered, in a place that is near.<sup>2</sup> Moses gives no address for it beyond the mouth and the heart. In the week before Rosh Hashana that is close enough to reach.</p>
