topic: "And the Lord remembered Sarah" — Rosh Hashana as Yom HaZikaron: the tradition that Sarah and Hannah were remembered on this day, and Hannah's whispered prayer (the first day's Torah reading and the haftarah of I Samuel 1) as the model for all our prayer

## Halakha

<p>The first day of Rosh Hashana falls this year on Shabbat, and no shofar is sounded. The Rambam rules that hearing the shofar is a positive commandment of the Torah, learned from the day of sounding named in the Torah reading for the festival.<sup>1</sup> He is candid about the difficulty. Blowing is restrained on Shabbat only by rabbinic decree, and a Torah commandment would ordinarily override such a restraint. The Sages ruled otherwise, out of concern that someone would carry a shofar through the public domain to a neighbor who could blow for him. All are obligated in the mitsvah; not all are skilled at it.<sup>2</sup> So we hear the thirty blasts on Sunday before Musaf, and then the blasts after Malkhiyot, Zikhronot and Shofarot.<sup>3</sup></p><p>Communities differ over the blast that follows Musaf. The Tur, in the name of Rav Amram Gaon, records a great teru'ah after the prayer with no tekia around it, to confuse the Satan. Rav Hai Gaon held that this was never a formal custom, only something individuals did, and that nothing is missing where it is left out, since the obligation has already been fulfilled. Older Ashkenazic books record full sets of blasts at that point. A Sephardic student of custom adds his own reason: as the congregation disperses, the Shekhina rises from among them, and the teru'ah rises with it.<sup>4</sup> The blast marks that the prayer is finished.</p>

## Aggada

<p>The Talmud preserves a tradition that on Rosh Hashana Sarah, Rachel and Hannah were remembered.<sup>1</sup> The midrash hears the word for remembering as a word from the world of deposits. The Holy One keeps safe what is entrusted; Sarah had left mitsvot and good deeds in that keeping, and on this day they were returned to her.<sup>2</sup> Hannah asks in the same language, that the Eternal look on her and remember her, and she prays with her lips moving and no voice heard. From her the Sages learned how prayer is made.<sup>3</sup> Yom HaZikaron means a day when what was entrusted is given back.</p>
