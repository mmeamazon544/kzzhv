## Halakha

<p>The Rambam rules that the Hanukkah lamp is kindled at sunset — not earlier, not later — and that one who has missed the hour may still light while there are passersby in the street, about half an hour.<sup>1</sup> This year the first light falls on Friday, when sunset is also the coming in of Shabbat. The Hanukkah lamp is kindled first, before the Shabbat candles, with oil enough to burn well into the night. The Rambam also rules that when a person can afford only one, the oil for the Hanukkah lamp comes before the wine for kiddush, because it makes the miracle known.<sup>2</sup></p><p>Communities differ over when the blessings are said. In London and Amsterdam all three blessings are recited on the first night before anything is kindled, and the two blessings on the nights after; in the Land of Israel, in the Sephardic East and in the cities of the Maghreb one says the blessing for kindling, begins to light, and says the rest while lighting.<sup>3</sup> The Sephardic order is to kindle the new light of that night first, then turn back and light the lights of the nights before.<sup>4</sup></p>

## Aggada

<p>Jacob was left alone, and the Torah does not say why. Rashi says he had forgotten some small jars and went back over the ford to fetch them.<sup>1</sup> The midrash fills in the scene: the one who wrestled with him first appeared as a herdsman, and the two crossed their flocks and went back together to see whether anything had been left behind.<sup>2</sup> Two sages, finishing their business at the gates of Tyre, turned back in the same way and found a bundle of silk; they said they had the practice from Jacob. In the week Hanukkah begins, a small flask nobody counted gives light for eight days.</p>
