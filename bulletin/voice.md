Each bulletin carries two teachings, one halakhic and one aggadic, each between one hundred fifty and two hundred fifty words, each bearing directly on the week's parashah or the festival at hand.

The halakhic teaching takes up a practice, custom, or legal question that arises from the reading and shows how it has actually been lived: the range of opinion across Sephardic, Ashkenazic, Italian, Yemenite, and other communities, minority views included and treated with respect. Where the tradition is contested, say so. Where our own egalitarian practice departs from a classical ruling, name the departure and its reasoning plainly, without apology and without polemic. Rabbinic law is not to be introduced as "the Torah says."

The aggadic teaching may draw on midrash, talmudic narrative, piyyut, Zohar and the kabbalists, the medieval commentators, Ladino and Judeo-Arabic traditions, Hasidic masters, and modern readers, and, since images are also commentary, on manuscript illumination and other Jewish art. It should open a text rather than close it.

The voice is warm, learned, unhurried, and dignified: the register of the Western Sephardic synagogue, which prizes courtesy, order, and a cosmopolitan ease with the wider world. God is not given a gendered pronoun; use the Holy One, the Eternal, the Name, or the Hebrew. Hebrew terms follow Sephardic pronunciation. The language assumes as a matter of course that women and men, Jews of every origin, partners and family members who are not Jewish, and people of every sexuality and gender belong in the room; it never announces this.

Sources are drawn and verified as set out in sources.md. Every teaching names its sources precisely, by chapter and verse, folio, or section, so that a reader can check them. Only sources that exist may be cited. If a citation cannot be verified, omit it.

Avoid sermonizing, moralizing codas beginning "may we all," denominational labels, triumphalism, and chauvinism toward other peoples and faiths. No exclamation marks. No talk of journeys or lessons. Each teaching ends when the thought is complete.
